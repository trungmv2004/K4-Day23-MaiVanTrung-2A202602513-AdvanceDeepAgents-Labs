# Survey on Efficient Inference and Small Language Models

## TL;DR
* Small language models (SLMs) achieve competitive data efficiency on single GPUs through 135M-parameter Llama-style architectures [1], while recursive Transformers decouple parameter count from per-token compute using shared blocks and factorized embeddings [2].
* Efficient inference relies on joint weight compression methods: Activation-Aware Weight Pruning (AWP) combines activation-aware pruning and quantization via projected gradient descent [3], whereas Optimal Brain Restoration (OBR) aligns quantization and sparsification using group error compensation [4].
* Data-centric training improves small language model performance across domain tasks: structured dataset filtering and educational token mixtures achieve 87.1% of baseline benchmark performance at 2.17% of token count [1], guided synthetic data generation enhances industrial diagnosis in 3B models [5], and importance sampling optimizes domain-specific pretraining perplexity [6].
* Edge deployment relies on targeted hardware-software co-design: Peripheral Language Models incorporate Multi-head Latent Attention and squared ReLU sparsity for ubiquitous devices [7], QMC combines outlier-aware quantization with heterogeneous MRAM/ReRAM memory architectures [8], and edge evaluation protocols benchmark compact models under shared-memory constraints [9].

## Background
Small Language Models (SLMs) refer to compact decoder-only Transformer architectures, typically spanning 100M to 5B parameters, designed to deliver high linguistic competence under strict computational, memory, and energy budgets [10]. Unlike web-scale language models that scale parameter counts across massive cluster infrastructure, modern SLMs focus on maximizing performance per parameter and per token. Foundational empirical studies demonstrate that state-of-the-art SLMs are heavily overtrained relative to classical Chinchilla scaling prescriptions, often consuming hundreds of billions to trillions of tokens [10]. Consequently, high-quality data curation—including deduplication, educational token filtering, and importance sampling—serves as a primary prerequisite for achieving competitive capabilities in compact models [1][6][10]. To enable execution on resource-constrained edge platforms, recent SLM research increasingly couples architectural sparsity and compact embedding structures with specialized hardware-software co-design [7][8][11].

## Inference Optimization Techniques
Post-training weight compression for small language models has evolved from isolated pruning or quantization steps into integrated joint compression frameworks. Activation-Aware Weight Pruning (AWP) unifies layer-wise activation-aware weight pruning and quantization through projected gradient descent, offering theoretical convergence guarantees while mitigating accuracy degradation in compressed models [3]. In parallel, Optimal Brain Restoration (OBR) provides a training-free framework that reconciles the conflicting distribution requirements of pruning and quantization; by optimizing a second-order Hessian objective and executing group error compensation, OBR enables aggressive W4A4KV4 quantization with 50% sparsity, delivering up to 4.72× speedups and 6.4× memory reductions compared to FP16 baselines [4].

For low-latency inference on hardware accelerators, algorithmic compression is frequently paired with speculative decoding. By combining a quantized target model with quantization-aware distillation, researchers recover high output accuracy while maintaining low precision grids [12]. Specially trained block-diffusion drafter models, adapted via two-stage distillation and enhanced with sliding-window attention, preserve draft-token acceptance rates and reduce long-context decoding latency, achieving a 6.978× average speedup on NVIDIA A10G GPUs [12].

## Architectural Innovations and Training Strategies
Architectural modifications in SLMs aim to decouple representational capability from raw parameter counts and per-token compute. Under data-constrained settings (e.g., 10M to 100M token budgets), standard Transformer scaling often leads to overfitting due to large vocabulary embedding tables; RecursiveGPT addresses this by combining factorized embeddings ($W_{\mathrm{emb}} \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times E}$ and $W_{\mathrm{proj}} \in \mathbb{R}^{E \times H}$) with shared recursive Transformer blocks, scaling depth and compute independently of embedding parameter count [2]. Furthermore, while embedding weight tying is widely used for parameter compression, empirical evaluation reveals that weight tying collapses input encoding and output prediction roles in sub-10M parameter models; the SLlama architecture addresses this limitation using Repeated Reduced Hidden Size and Projection (RRHP) and permutated weight attention, achieving a 31.72% gain in linguistic competence over tied baselines under strict token limits [11].

Domain specialization leverages structured data pipelines and synthetic data generation. DiagnosticSLM (a 3B-parameter model) combines Domain-Adaptive Pretraining (DAPT), Domain-specific Supervised Fine-tuning (DSFT), and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with guided synthetic data augmentation from Gemma-2-27B and MinHash fuzzy deduplication, improving industrial diagnostic accuracy by up to 25% over baseline open-source models [5]. When adapting pretrained models to specialized domains under budget limits, cluster-based importance sampling yields superior domain perplexity for single-task specialization, whereas Projected Networks offer reduced training overhead across multi-domain setups through shared pretraining costs [6]. Single-GPU studies such as L20-Edu-135M demonstrate that a 135M parameter model trained on 13B educational and reasoning tokens reaches 87.1% of SmolLM-135M's mean zero-shot score while using only 2.17% as many pretraining tokens [1].

## Deployment Challenges at the Edge
Deploying SLMs on edge platforms (e.g., mobile SoCs, single-board computers like Raspberry Pi, and embedded GPUs) presents severe bottlenecks in memory bandwidth and available RAM (typically restricted to 1–8 GB shared memory) [9]. Comprehensive evaluations across 60+ SLMs confirm that while compact models match 7B parameters on general benchmarks, their in-context learning remains constrained and execution efficiency requires aggressive dynamic routing and KV cache compression [10].

Hardware-software co-design addresses these memory bandwidth limits through specialized activation patterns and heterogeneous memory layouts. Peripheral Language Models (PLM) integrate Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA) and squared ReLU activations to encourage activation sparsity, reducing peak memory footprints during inference across consumer GPUs, mobile phones, and Raspberry Pi devices [7]. For non-volatile memory architectures, Outlier-aware Quantization with Memory Co-design (QMC) partitions weight tensors into inliers and outliers; inlier weights are stored in compact multi-level ReRAM, while critical high-magnitude outliers are maintained in high-precision on-chip MRAM, mitigating device noise and achieving 5.5×–7.3× memory reductions, 11×–11.7× energy savings, and 9.4×–12.5× latency reductions on edge AI platforms such as NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin [8].

## Trends and open problems
Recent developments (2024–2026) emphasize data-centric curation, joint pruning-quantization algorithms, and hardware-software co-design as core pillars for compact language model deployment [1][3][8][4]. Data quality filtering and synthetic dataset generation consistently demonstrate higher efficiency returns than parameter expansion alone [1][5][10].

Several critical open problems remain active areas of investigation:
* **Reinforcement Learning Bottlenecks at Small Scale:** Direct GRPO-style RLVR on 135M parameter models decreases exact-match accuracy on reasoning tasks (GSM8K), indicating that post-training reasoning alignment methods developed for large models do not transfer naively to sub-1B architectures [1].
* **In-Context Learning Capabilities:** Small language models exhibit limited in-context learning capabilities relative to their overall task performance, restricting their effectiveness in complex dynamic task-routing workflows [10].
* **Hardware Availability and Simulation Gaps:** Promising co-design approaches such as QMC rely on heterogeneous non-volatile memory hierarchies (MRAM/ReRAM) that are not yet broadly available in commodity mobile SoCs, necessitating further software-only optimizations for standard edge hardware [8][9].
* **Synthetic Data Generation Overhead:** Guided synthetic data pipelines (e.g., DiagnosticSLM) require significant compute (~5,400 GPU-hours for augmentation) and risk propagating synthetic biases from large teacher models to compact downstream SLMs [5].
